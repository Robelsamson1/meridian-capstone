## National level — foot traffic and share

Source: [[sources/jll-grocery-tracker-2026]]

- Traditional supermarkets' foot traffic share fell from 75.1% (2019) to
  73.2%, while two categories gained share: value operators (Aldi, Grocery
  Outlet) and fresh-format specialists (Trader Joe's, Whole Foods,
  Sprouts).
- Foot traffic growth by banner: Trader Joe's +10.4%, Whole Foods +9.8%,
  Aldi +8.3%, Kroger +0.3%.
- Aldi opened 180 U.S. stores in 2025 and plans 180 more in 2026.

## National level — where new stores are physically located

Source: [[sources/jll-grocery-tracker-2026]]

- Of 400 new grocery store openings tracked in 2025, 215 (over half) were
  in the Southeast, led by Florida (cited drivers: population migration,
  business-friendly policies, consumer spending).
- A secondary cluster is forming in the Mid-Atlantic (New Jersey, Maryland,
  DC/Baltimore corridor).

## National level — chain-specific growth and format innovation

Source: [[sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026]]

- Trader Joe's grew 3% YoY (period ending Feb. 28), 6 points ahead of the
  broader sector, with especially strong Gen Z growth — while Publix and
  Safeway saw pullbacks.
- Aldi is piloting a modular, smaller-urban-footprint store format
  (launched Aventura, FL, Sept. 2025).
- Gelson's opened "ReCharge" micromarkets (3,000-5,400 sq. ft.) pairing
  groceries with EV charging and fast-casual food, in Southern California.
- Grocery-anchored real estate transactions reached ~$11B in 2025 (+42%
  YoY), suggesting investor appetite for sites these formats occupy.

## Local/LA level — an actual store-by-store example

Source: [[sources/bristol-farms-vs-erewhon-2026]]

- [[entities/bristol-farms]], a Southern California specialty grocer, is
  expanding within Los Angeles itself: 13 current locations, a 14th
  opening in Hollywood mid-April 2026.
- It's positioned explicitly as a lower-priced alternative to
  [[entities/erewhon]] within the same LA specialty-grocer market.

## My own conclusion (not stated by any source)

None of the three sources says anything about Pasadena, Meridian, or
Southern California specialty-grocer growth specifically — the national
data (JLL/ICSC) concentrates openings in the Southeast/Mid-Atlantic, and
the one Southern California data point (Hoodline) is a single competitor
opening in Hollywood, not a market-wide trend. **Our current sources
support "specialty grocers are winning nationally" but don't yet support
any claim about specialty-grocer growth being concentrated in, or absent
from, the LA/Pasadena market specifically** — that's a gap, not something
the sources answer. See [[analyses/open-questions]].
