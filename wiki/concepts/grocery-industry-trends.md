# Grocery industry trends (2026)

Source: [[sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026]]

## Market stratification

The U.S. grocery sector (~$912B annually) is stratifying: "the days when
any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading."
Discount grocers, traditional supermarkets, and specialty formats are
diverging in performance rather than moving together.

## Specialty grocers are outperforming

Trader Joe's — a specialty grocer, the same category
[[entities/meridian-markets]] competes in — grew 3% YoY, 6 points ahead of
the broader sector, with especially strong Gen Z growth, while
conventional chains like Publix and Safeway saw pullbacks. This is
external validation of the specialty-grocer strategy described in
[[concepts/prepared-foods-positioning]], not proof it applies to Meridian
specifically.

## Small-format and flexible-footprint innovation

Aldi is piloting a modular format built for smaller urban footprints, and
Gelson's has opened 3,000-5,400 sq. ft. "ReCharge" micromarkets. Both are
national/regional players actively investing in the smaller-footprint
approach [[entities/meridian-markets]] already uses — relevant context for
[[concepts/store-expansion-strategy]] and any Pasadena site-format
decision.

## Investment activity

Grocery-anchored real estate transactions reached ~$11B in 2025, up 42%
from 2024, with multiple large institutional buyers (Brixton Capital,
Medipower, Nuveen, Blackstone) active in the space. This suggests investor
appetite for grocery-anchored retail real estate is currently strong —
relevant context for lease negotiations on a new site.

## Restaurant/foodservice competition

A 2026 Tillster survey found consumers shifting dining occasions toward
grocery and convenience stores and away from fast food/fast-casual. This
directly supports the strategic importance of
[[concepts/prepared-foods-positioning]] as a growth lever, not just a
point of differentiation.

## Relevance to Meridian

This article doesn't mention Meridian Markets directly — it's general
industry context, not a client-specific source. It's useful as external
benchmarking: Meridian's specialty/small-footprint/prepared-foods strategy
matches what's currently outperforming nationally, and larger players are
now investing to catch up to formats Meridian already runs. Whether
Meridian is capturing this tailwind, or exposed to the same competitive
pressure from Aldi/Gelson's-style small formats, is untested — see
[[analyses/open-questions]].
