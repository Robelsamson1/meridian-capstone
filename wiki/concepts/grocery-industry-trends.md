# Grocery industry trends (2026)

Source: [[sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026]], [[sources/jll-grocery-tracker-2026]]

## Market stratification

The U.S. grocery sector (~$912B annually) is stratifying: "the days when
any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading."
Discount grocers, traditional supermarkets, and specialty formats are
diverging in performance rather than moving together.

## Specialty grocers are outperforming

Trader Joe's — a specialty grocer, the same category
[[entities/meridian-markets]] competes in — grew 3% YoY, 6 points ahead of
the broader sector, with especially strong Gen Z growth, while
conventional chains like Publix and Safeway saw pullbacks. This is
external validation of the specialty-grocer strategy described in
[[concepts/prepared-foods-positioning]], not proof it applies to Meridian
specifically.

A second source corroborates and sharpens this: traditional supermarkets'
foot traffic share fell from 75.1% (2019) to 73.2%, while two categories
gained — value operators (Aldi, Grocery Outlet) and fresh-format
specialists (Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, Sprouts). Foot traffic growth:
Trader Joe's +10.4%, Whole Foods +9.8%, Aldi +8.3%, Kroger +0.3%. This
"barbell" pattern — value at one end, fresh-format specialty at the
other, conventional supermarkets squeezed in the middle — is the clearest
national signal yet that Meridian's specialty positioning sits in one of
the two growth lanes, not the shrinking middle.

## The value-conscious consumer

Food costs are up 30%+ since 2020 and private label now exceeds 21% of
grocery spending ($282.8B), up 30% since 2021 — nearly half of shoppers
actively choose store brand over name brand to save money. Shoppers are
also making more frequent, shorter "bite-sized" trips across multiple
stores rather than one large weekly trip. This matters for Meridian on two
fronts: it's underlying evidence for why value-format competitors (Aldi,
Grocery Outlet) are expanding aggressively, and it raises a question about
whether Meridian's own private-label mix and basket-size assumptions still
hold — see [[analyses/open-questions]].

## Small-format and flexible-footprint innovation

Aldi is piloting a modular format built for smaller urban footprints, and
Gelson's has opened 3,000-5,400 sq. ft. "ReCharge" micromarkets. Both are
national/regional players actively investing in the smaller-footprint
approach [[entities/meridian-markets]] already uses — relevant context for
[[concepts/store-expansion-strategy]] and any Pasadena site-format
decision.

## Investment activity

Grocery-anchored real estate transactions reached ~$11B in 2025, up 42%
from 2024, with multiple large institutional buyers (Brixton Capital,
Medipower, Nuveen, Blackstone) active in the space. This suggests investor
appetite for grocery-anchored retail real estate is currently strong —
relevant context for lease negotiations on a new site.

Grocery-anchored centers also show structurally tighter fundamentals than
retail generally: 4.0% vacancy vs. 6.3% for non-anchored centers, a 4.4%
NNN rent premium, and average cap rates of 6.7% (down ~40bps from the 2023
peak) with institutional buyers now at a decade-high 27% share of deals.
Rents held up even through COVID-era disruption. This is favorable
landlord-side context for [[concepts/store-expansion-strategy]] — a
grocery-anchored lease is currently a seller's/landlord's market nationally,
which is worth confirming (or discounting) for the Pasadena submarket
specifically.

## Geographic concentration of new openings

Of 400 new store openings tracked nationally in 2025, 215 (over half) were
in the Southeast, led by Florida, with a secondary Mid-Atlantic cluster
(New Jersey, Maryland, DC/Baltimore). Meridian operates in LA/Orange/
Ventura counties, outside both named growth clusters — this doesn't mean
Southern California isn't a viable expansion market, but it means the
national growth narrative isn't a direct proxy for local site quality in
Pasadena. See [[analyses/open-questions]].

## E-commerce and fulfillment

Online grocery is projected to reach $228B by 2030 (23%+ of total grocery
spend), with home delivery now 45% of online orders — ahead of pickup and
shipping. Newer grocery real estate is increasingly expected to
accommodate delivery staging areas and flexible fulfillment-plus-retail
layouts. Whether a Pasadena site needs to plan for this, and whether
Meridian currently offers delivery at all, is untracked — see
[[analyses/open-questions]].

## Restaurant/foodservice competition

A 2026 Tillster survey found consumers shifting dining occasions toward
grocery and convenience stores and away from fast food/fast-casual. This
directly supports the strategic importance of
[[concepts/prepared-foods-positioning]] as a growth lever, not just a
point of differentiation.

## Relevance to Meridian

Neither article mentions Meridian Markets directly — this is general
industry context, not a client-specific source. It's useful as external
benchmarking: Meridian's specialty/small-footprint/prepared-foods strategy
matches what's currently outperforming nationally, and larger players are
now investing to catch up to formats Meridian already runs. Whether
Meridian is capturing this tailwind, or exposed to the same competitive
pressure from Aldi/Gelson's-style small formats, is untested — see
[[analyses/open-questions]].
