## Summary

Industry trade article on the "barbell" divergence in U.S. grocery:
value operators (Aldi, Grocery Outlet) and fresh-format specialty grocers
(Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, Sprouts) are gaining foot traffic and share
while traditional supermarkets stagnate, against a backdrop of persistent
grocery-anchored real estate strength and continued e-commerce growth. See
[[concepts/grocery-industry-trends]] for the synthesis and why it matters
for this engagement.

## Key facts

- Food costs up 30%+ since 2020; ~80% of shoppers report noticing price
  increases. Market has bifurcated into a "barbell" of premium-spending
  high-income households and value-seeking middle/lower-income households.
- Private label now over 21% of grocery spending ($282.8B), up 30% since
  2021; shoppers increasingly make frequent, short "bite-sized" trips
  across multiple stores to hunt deals.
- Traditional supermarkets' foot traffic share fell from 75.1% (2019) to
  73.2%. Winners: value operators (Aldi, Grocery Outlet) and fresh-format
  specialists (Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, Sprouts).
- Foot traffic growth: Trader Joe's +10.4%, Whole Foods +9.8%, Aldi +8.3%,
  Kroger +0.3%. Aldi opened 180 U.S. stores in 2025, plans 180 more in
  2026.
- Grocery-anchored real estate: 4.0% vacancy (vs. 6.3% non-anchored), 4.4%
  NNN rent premium, ~$11B in 2025 transactions (+42% YoY), institutional
  buyer share at a decade-high 27%, average cap rates 6.7% (down ~40bps
  from 2023 peak). Rents grew even through COVID-era disruption.
- New store openings in 2025 concentrated geographically: 215 of 400
  tracked openings in the Southeast, led by Florida; a secondary
  Mid-Atlantic cluster (NJ, MD, DC/Baltimore).
- Online grocery projected to reach $228B by 2030 (23%+ of total grocery
  spend); home delivery now 45% of online orders, ahead of pickup/shipping
  — pushing new format requirements (delivery staging areas, flexible
  fulfillment-plus-retail layouts).
