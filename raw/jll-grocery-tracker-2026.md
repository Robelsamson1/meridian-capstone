## The Barbell Shopper

Food costs have risen over 30% since 2020. Nearly 80% of shoppers report
noticing supermarket price increases. The market has bifurcated into
high-income households spending on premium products and middle/lower-income
families prioritizing value, creating a "barbell" distribution where the
middle is squeezed. Credit card debt and drained savings among middle- and
lower-income families are forcing value prioritization across the market.

## Private Label Surge

Store brands have grown 30% since 2021, now representing over 21% of
grocery spending ($282.8 billion). Nearly half of surveyed shoppers
actively choose store brands over name brands for savings. Shopping
behavior has shifted toward frequent, shorter "bite-sized trips" across
multiple stores to hunt deals.

## The Grocery Divergence

Traditional supermarkets' foot traffic share dropped from 75.1% (2019) to
73.2%, losing billions in sales. Two categories are winning:

- **Value operators**: Aldi and Grocery Outlet.
- **Fresh-format specialists**: Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, Sprouts.

Foot traffic growth by banner: Kroger +0.3%, Trader Joe's +10.4%, Whole
Foods +9.8%, Aldi +8.3%. Aldi opened 180 stores in 2025 and plans 180 more
in 2026.

## Canadian Market Parallels

Canadian grocers are expanding discount formats: Loblaw (No Frills, Maxi),
Empire (FreshCo), and Metro (Super C, Food Basics) — all responding to
persistent price sensitivity.

## Real Estate Metrics

- Grocery-anchored vacancy: 4.0% (vs. 6.3% for non-anchored retail).
- NNN rent premium for grocery-anchored centers: 4.4%.
- Current availability: 4.7%.
- 2025 transaction volume: nearly $11 billion (42% increase YoY).
- Institutional investor share of transactions: 27% (highest in a decade).
- Average cap rates (end of 2025): 6.7%, down ~40 basis points from the
  2023 peak.
- Historical resilience: during COVID, despite a 68% drop in new
  deliveries and four quarters of negative net absorption, grocery-anchored
  rents still grew 1.5%, and availability increased less than 1 percentage
  point before tightening again.

## Geographic Concentration

The Southeast dominates new store openings, with 215 of 400 tracked new
openings in 2025. Florida leads due to population migration,
business-friendly policies, and strong consumer spending. Mid-Atlantic
clusters are forming in New Jersey, Maryland, and the Washington
D.C./Baltimore corridor — high-density, affluent markets.

## E-Commerce Evolution

Online grocery is projected to reach $228 billion by 2030 (over 23% of
total grocery spending). Home delivery now represents 45% of online
orders, surpassing pickup and shipping. This shift requires properties to
accommodate delivery staging areas and flexible layouts serving both retail
and fulfillment functions simultaneously.
